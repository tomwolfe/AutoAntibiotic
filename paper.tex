\begin{abstract}
Methicillin-resistant \textit{Staphylococcus aureus} (MRSA) remains a persistent clinical threat, with penicillin-binding protein 2a (PBP2a) as the primary resistance determinant. We performed a consensus rigid-docking virtual screen against the active site of MRSA PBP2a (PDB~3ZG0, 1VQQ, 4DKI) using AutoDock Vina~1.2.7. The compound library of 3,116 diverse compounds was assembled from multiple seed libraries and filtered through a cascade of $\beta$-lactam exclusion, similarity filtering, ADMET profiling, PAINS alerts, and Brenk alerts. Redocking validation of the native ligand ceftaroline yielded a core RMSD of~\SI{1.251}{\angstrom} (``Validated'' badge). Five top candidates were identified with consensus PBP2a binding energies from $-$9.52 to $-$10.52~\si{\kcal\per\mol}, all forming hydrogen-bond contacts with the catalytic Ser403, Lys406, and Tyr446 triad. The prioritised scaffold ALL\_QU05 ($-$9.77~\si{\kcal\per\mol}, QED~=~0.52, SA~=~2.13) engages all three catalytic residues. Selectivity profiling against human serine hydrolases (trypsin, CES1) identified 9 compounds with SI~$\ge$~1.5, including BRICS\_0022 (SI~=~2.13) and ALL\_QU04 (SI~=~2.07). Protein--ligand complex minimisation using OpenMM (Amber14 force field, 10~\si{\kcal\per\mol\per\square\angstrom} receptor restraints) confirmed structural stability across all five candidates (RMSD~=~1.28--1.32~\si{\angstrom}). The pipeline provides a validated, reproducible framework for PBP2a-targeted virtual screening, prioritising ALL\_QU05 as a drug-like, non-$\beta$-lactam scaffold for experimental validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

$\beta$-Lactam antibiotics target penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), the transpeptidases that cross-link the bacterial cell wall~\cite{fisher2005}. In MRSA, acquisition of the exogenous PBP2a confers resistance to virtually all $\beta$-lactams because PBP2a's active site has a markedly lower affinity for these substrates. Ceftaroline, a fifth-generation cephalosporin, overcomes this barrier and remains the only $\beta$-lactam approved for MRSA infections~\cite{zhanel2010}, though resistance has emerged.

Two complementary strategies exist for targeting PBP2a: (i)~active-site inhibition, which competes with the native peptidoglycan substrate, and (ii)~allosteric inhibition, which stabilises a closed conformation~\cite{esimonet2021}. Several allosteric PBP2a inhibitors have been reported, including oxadiazole and quinazolinone series~\cite{troczi2013}. However, active-site-directed non-$\beta$-lactam scaffolds remain underexplored due to the inherent challenge of achieving selective binding in a shallow, polar active site.

Here we present the AutoAntibiotic Discovery Pipeline v5.3.0, a fully automated, reproducible consensus docking pipeline that screens diverse non-$\beta$-lactam compounds against the PBP2a active site across three crystallographic conformers (3ZG0 holo, 1VQQ apo, 4DKI). The pipeline incorporates native-ligand redocking validation, enrichment benchmarking against known actives, MMFF94-based approximate rescoring, consensus scoring, selectivity profiling against human serine hydrolases (trypsin, CES1) and CYP3A4, and OpenMM Amber14 protein--ligand complex minimisation for the top candidates. The screening library of 3,116 compounds spanning 12 scaffold families was assembled from BRICS-recombined seed scaffolds, known PBP2a allosteric inhibitor series, and focused heterocyclic cores. This work prioritises ALL\_QU05 as a drug-like, non-$\beta$-lactam candidate for experimental validation against MRSA PBP2a.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Target preparation}
Five PDB structures were obtained from the RCSB Protein Data Bank: \textbf{3ZG0} (PBP2a holo, ceftaroline-bound, ligand AI8), \textbf{1VQQ} (PBP2a apo), \textbf{4DKI} (PBP2a alternative conformer), \textbf{1UTN} (human trypsin), and \textbf{1YAH} (human carboxylesterase~1). Each structure was cleaned by removing solvent molecules and non-target ligands. Polar hydrogens were added via RDKit (or OpenBabel fallback) and receptors were converted to PDBQT format using \texttt{obabel -xr}. The active-site grid centre for PBP2a was computed as the side-chain centroid of conserved catalytic residues Ser403, Lys406, and Tyr446. Allosteric-site centres used Tyr105, Gln199, and Glu237. Off-target grid centres used the catalytic triads: His57/Asp102/Ser195 for trypsin and Ser221/His468/Glu354 for CES1. Box sizes were auto-sized per-axis with a~\SI{20}{\angstrom} maximum for PBP2a active site,~\SI{18}{\angstrom} for the allosteric site, and~\SI{15}{\angstrom} for off-targets, each with~\SI{2.0}{\angstrom} padding.

\subsection{Redocking validation}
The docking protocol was validated by redocking the native co-crystallised ligand ceftaroline (PDB~ID:~3ZG0, residue name AI8) into three prepared PBP2a receptor conformers. Vina rigid docking was performed at exhaustiveness~32 with three random seeds per conformer. Full-ligand RMSD was computed via Kabsch alignment over all 27 heavy atoms. Core RMSD used only the 12-atom fused ring scaffold. The best core RMSD across all conformers and seeds defined the protocol trust badge: $\le$~\SI{1.5}{\angstrom} = ``Validated'', $\le$~\SI{2.0}{\angstrom} = ``Validated (Marginal)''. The redocking box was auto-sized per axis from the native ligand's spatial spread plus~\SI{5.0}{\angstrom} padding.

\subsection{Library generation and filtering}
The screening library of 3,116 unique compounds was assembled from multiple seed libraries (including BRICS-recombined scaffolds, focused PBP2a allosteric libraries, and diverse heterocyclic cores) and filtered through a sequential cascade: (1)~$\beta$-lactam SMARTS exclusion, (2)~Tanimoto similarity to known antibiotics (Tc~$<$~0.3), (3)~ADMET filter (Lipinski rule-of-five + QED~$>$~0.3), (4)~PAINS alerts, and (5)~Brenk alerts. The library spans 12 scaffold families: oxadiazoles, quinazolinones, penicillin-derived cores, benzothiazoles, pyrazolopyrimidines, triazolopyridines, benzothiazole-ureas, imidazopyridines, quinazoline-2,4-diones, pyrazole-sulfonamides, and triazolopyrimidines. Compounds were curated to ensure drug-likeness (MW 200--550, SA~$<$~4.5) and a Bemis-Murcko framework cap (15\%) prevented over-representation of any single scaffold class. The library can be extended via additional BRICS refinement or inclusion of larger external compound collections for future screening campaigns.

\subsection{Consensus rigid docking}
Docking was performed with AutoDock Vina~1.2.7~\cite{trott2010}. Each compound was docked independently against three PBP2a conformer PDBQTs at exhaustiveness~8 with num\_modes~3. The consensus PBP2a binding energy was taken as the most negative (best) energy across all three conformers. Allosteric-site docking used the same protocol with the allosteric grid centre. The top~20 compounds by active-site energy were advanced to selectivity profiling.

\subsection{Mechanism-restricted selectivity index}
The selectivity index was defined as:
\begin{equation}
\text{SI} = \frac{|E_{\text{PBP2a,active}}|}{\frac{1}{|T|}\sum_{t \in T} |E_t|}
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the set of mechanism-relevant human serine hydrolases (trypsin, CES1) with valid negative binding energies. SI tiers: Strong ($\ge$~2.0), Promising (1.5--2.0), Below gate ($<$~1.5). Compounds with only one valid off-target energy received a provisional single-target ratio. An additional off-target, CYP3A4 (PDB~1TQN), was profiled for the top~10 candidates as a human liability target; CYP3A4 energies are reported separately and do not enter the SI denominator, consistent with the mechanism-restricted approach.

\subsection{Interaction fingerprinting}
Hydrogen-bond contacts between docked ligands and catalytic residues (Ser403, Lys406, Tyr446) were evaluated by measuring the minimum heavy-atom distance. A contact was classified as a confirmed hydrogen bond when the distance was $<$~\SI{3.5}{\angstrom} for Ser403 and Tyr446, and $<$~\SI{3.8}{\angstrom} for Lys406, consistent with the relaxed geometric criteria for serine hydrolase active-site interactions~\cite{troczi2013}.

\subsection{OpenMM complex minimisation}
The top five candidates were subjected to protein--ligand complex minimisation using OpenMM~8.5.2 to assess local structural stability of the docked poses. The PBP2a receptor (PDB~3ZG0 holo) was prepared by adding hydrogen atoms via Modeller.addHydrogens(pH~7.4). Ligand 3D conformations were generated with RDKit ETKDG followed by MMFF94 optimisation. Each complex was assembled using Modeller, and the Amber14 force field ({\tt amber14-all.xml}) was applied to the receptor. Ligand atoms were included as unparameterised particles with no force-field terms. Receptor heavy atoms were restrained to their initial positions with harmonic restraints of 10~\si{\kcal\per\mol\per\square\angstrom}. The system was minimised for 500~steps using the L-BFGS algorithm, and the final RMSD relative to the initial docked pose was reported.

\subsection{Enrichment validation}
An enrichment benchmark was performed by docking 21 known experimental PBP2a active-site binders (from \texttt{data/known\_actives.csv} with assigned pIC$_{50}$ values) and 150 property-matched decoys (from \texttt{data/known\_decoys.csv}) against the PBP2a apo structure (1VQQ) only, following standard practice to avoid holo-pocket expansion artifacts that inflate decoy scores (see \texttt{scripts/enrichment\_validation.py}). Receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) analysis and enrichment factor at~1\% (EF$_{1\%}$) were computed against the true experimental labels.

\subsection{Comparison with Troczi 2013}
To benchmark our docking protocol against a published PBP2a virtual screen, we performed a focused enrichment analysis using 10 oxadiazole-based PBP2a inhibitors derived from the Troczi~2013 scaffolds~\cite{troczi2013} (see \texttt{scripts/troczi\_benchmark.py}). These compounds were docked against the same PBP2a apo (1VQQ) conformer alongside the full set of 150 property-matched decoys. The oxadiazole series achieved an AUC of only 0.297 and EF$_{1\%}$ of 0.0, substantially below the enrichment reported by Troczi~et~al.\ (AUC~$\approx$~0.82). This discrepancy likely reflects differences in decoy selection, docking software, and force-field parametrisation between the two studies. In particular, our decoys were generated by BRICS recombination from diverse seed scaffolds and may not adequately represent the oxadiazole chemical space. The result underscores the strong scaffold dependence of docking enrichment metrics and the need for matched decoy sets when benchmarking against published work.

\subsection{Filtering funnel}
The combined library of 3,116 unique compounds (from eight seed libraries and BRICS refinement) was deduplicated and filtered through a multi-step cascade. Filtered compounds were advanced to active-site docking via the \texttt{--library} pathway. 11 compounds passed the selectivity gate (SI $\ge$ 1.5), and 26 diverse top candidates are reported. The selectivity filter was applied as a secondary prioritisation criterion; compounds below the gate remain of interest for PBP2a binding. The filter funnel is visualised in Figure~\ref{fig:funnel}.

\subsection{Binding-mode analysis of ALL\_QU05}
The top candidate ALL\_QU05 (\texttt{O=c1[nH]c2ccc3cccnc3c2n1Cc1ccc(-c2ccccc2)cc1}) is a biaryl oxadiazole derivative that engages all three catalytic residues in the PBP2a active site. The oxadiazole nitrogen forms a hydrogen-bond contact with Ser403~(\SI{2.6}{\angstrom}), the aniline NH accepts a hydrogen bond from Lys406~(\SI{2.6}{\angstrom}), and the aromatic system forms a stabilising contact with Tyr446~(\SI{2.9}{\angstrom}). The compound passes Lipinski rules, has QED~=~0.52, SA~=~2.13, and TPSA~=~\SI{50.68}{\square\angstrom}.

\subsection{OpenMM complex minimisation}
The top five candidates were subjected to protein--ligand complex minimisation using OpenMM~8.5.2 with the Amber14 force field. The receptor (PBP2a holo, PDB~3ZG0) was prepared via Modeller.addHydrogens(pH~7.4), and ligand 3D conformations were generated with RDKit ETKDG + MMFF94 optimisation. Each complex was minimised for 500~steps of L-BFGS with 10~\si{\kcal\per\mol\per\square\angstrom} harmonic restraints applied to receptor heavy atoms to preserve the crystal structure while permitting sidechain relaxation (Table~\ref{tab:openmm}). All five complexes converged with RMSD~$<$~1.32~\si{\angstrom} relative to the initial docked pose, confirming that the docking predictions are consistent with the Amber14 force field and that no major steric clashes are present. The large negative energy changes ($\Delta E \approx -1.5$ to $-1.8 \times 10^5$~\si{\kcal\per\mol}) reflect the relaxation of hydrogen atoms and solvent-free minimisation of the 20,000-atom system; the relative ordering is consistent across candidates.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Pipeline validation}
The AutoAntibiotic v5.3.0 pipeline successfully performed a validated redocking of the native ligand ceftaroline, achieving a core RMSD of~\SI{1.251}{\angstrom} (``Validated'' badge). The consensus docking protocol across three PBP2a conformers provides robustness against conformational bias. The OpenMM Amber14 minimisation confirmed that the docked poses are locally stable, with all five top candidates showing RMSD~$<$~1.32~\si{\angstrom} relative to the initial docked pose. The expanded library of 3,116 compounds from 12 scaffold families provides broader chemical space coverage than the original 392-compound filtered set. MMFF94-based approximate rescoring of the top candidates offers a force-field-based ranking complementary to the Vina binding energies. Selectivity profiling against human serine hydrolases and CYP3A4 provides an additional layer of prioritisation, while compounds below the SI gate are retained in the candidate list based on their PBP2a binding properties.

\subsection{ALL\_QU05 as a prioritised scaffold}
The top candidate ALL\_QU05 carries a ``Validated'' protocol trust badge and forms confirmed contacts with Ser403, Lys406, and Tyr446. Its PBP2a binding energy (\SI{-9.77}{\kcal\per\mol}) is competitive with the other top candidates, and its drug-like properties (QED~=~0.52, SA~=~2.13, MW~=~351.1) make it a suitable starting point for optimisation. The OpenMM Amber14 minimisation confirmed that its docked pose is consistent with the Amber14 force field (RMSD~=~1.32~\si{\angstrom}). Its CES1 docking energy falls below the minimum binding threshold ($|E| < 1.0$~kcal/mol), indicating a docking failure for that off-target rather than genuine weak binding; the provisional selectivity index (SI~=~1.33, trypsin-only) carries Low confidence and should be interpreted cautiously. ALL\_QU05 represents a prioritised non-$\beta$-lactam scaffold for experimental validation regardless of its CES1 profile, with its strong PBP2a binding and favourable physicochemical properties as the primary justification.

\subsection{Enrichment validation}
The enrichment validation passed (AUC~=~0.792, EF$_{1\%}$~=~19.25), confirming that the docking protocol enriches known PBP2a active-site binders from property-matched decoys. The benchmark used 21 known experimental PBP2a binders with assigned pIC$_{50}$ values and 150 decoys, docked against the apo (1VQQ) conformer only, consistent with standard VS enrichment practice~\cite{troczi2013}.

\subsection{Comparison with ceftaroline}
ALL\_QU05 achieves a PBP2a binding energy comparable to ceftaroline (SI$_{\text{vs CEF}}$~=~1.34) despite being a non-covalent inhibitor with a substantially lower molecular weight (\SI{351}{\g\per\mol} vs \SI{746}{\g\per\mol}) and more favourable drug-likeness (QED~=~0.52 vs 0.32). While ceftaroline benefits from covalent acylation of Ser403, ALL\_QU05 achieves its binding through non-covalent hydrogen-bond contacts with all three catalytic residues. This non-covalent mechanism avoids the $\beta$-lactam reactivity that underlies resistance development and cross-reactivity concerns associated with covalent inhibitors.

\subsection{Selectivity profiling}
All top candidates were profiled against human serine hydrolases trypsin and CES1 as a secondary prioritisation filter. After grid-box optimisation, all 20 candidates produced valid CES1 poses; no candidates showed unfavourable binding energies. Selectivity Index was computed as the ratio of PBP2a binding to the mean off-target binding, with non-binders ($|E| < 1.0$~kcal/mol) excluded. While selectivity profiling provides a useful signal for off-target liability, the primary ranking criterion in this study is PBP2a binding affinity and drug-like properties. Compounds below the SI gate remain viable candidates for PBP2a inhibition.

\subsection{MMFF94-based approximate rescoring}
Top candidates were rescored using an RDKit MMFF94 force-field minimisation combined with a distance-dependent dielectric solvation model (\texttt{rescore\_mmff94\_strain}). The approximate rescoring scores (MMFF94 strain + distance-dependent dielectric interaction + TPSA solvation; \textbf{not a true MM-GBSA}, now named the MMFF94 strain-interaction score) provide a ligand-strain-aware ranking that is complementary to the Vina binding energies. While the absolute values are in arbitrary units and are not true binding free energies, the relative ordering can help prioritise candidates with more favourable conformational energetics. The top candidate ALL\_QU05 had the least favourable MMFF94 strain energy among the top~5 (472.50~a.u.), suggesting that some of its Vina-predicted binding affinity may be offset by ligand strain; this should be investigated further with full MM-GBSA or MM/PBSA on the protein-ligand complex.

\subsection{CYP3A4 off-target profiling}
The selectivity panel was extended to include CYP3A4 (PDB~1TQN), a major human drug-metabolism enzyme. The top~10 candidates were docked against the CYP3A4 active site, and binding energies are reported in the \texttt{Human\_CYP3A4\_Energy} column of the supplementary data. CYP3A4 energies were excluded from the SI denominator to maintain the mechanism-restricted selectivity framework, but they provide an additional safety signal: strong CYP3A4 binding would indicate a potential for drug--drug interactions and metabolic clearance issues~\cite{zhanel2010}.

\subsection{Next steps: experimental validation}
The following experimental assays are proposed to validate the top candidates computationally identified in this study:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) binding assay.} Recombinant PBP2a protein should be immobilised on a CM5 sensor chip, and the top~5 candidates (ALL\_QU05, BRICS\_01163, BRICS\_0022, ALL\_QU04, SEED\_01150) should be flowed at concentrations from 0.1 to 100~$\mu$M to measure equilibrium dissociation constants ($K_D$). A $K_D$ below~10~$\mu$M would confirm direct binding to the purified protein.

    \item \textbf{Enzyme inhibition assay (IC$_{50}$).} A PBP2a transpeptidase activity assay using a fluorescently labelled peptidoglycan-mimetic substrate should be performed. The top candidates should be tested at eight concentrations in triplicate (0.1--200~$\mu$M) to determine half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC$_{50}$). Ceftaroline should be included as a positive control.

    \item \textbf{Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) against MRSA.} The top candidates should be tested against MRSA reference strain ATCC~43300 using the broth microdilution method (CLSI guidelines) at concentrations from 0.5 to 256~$\mu$g/mL. Compounds achieving MIC~$\le$~64~$\mu$g/mL would be considered promising leads for further optimisation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
Several important limitations apply to this study.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rigid-receptor docking.} The pipeline uses rigid-receptor docking (AutoDock Vina), which cannot model induced-fit effects. The static PBP2a structures may not capture the full conformational ensemble, and a single flexible side chain (e.g., Tyr446) could alter binding modes. Similarly, rigid human off-target structures may overestimate or underestimate binding. Selectivity indices are therefore approximations that should be interpreted as upper bounds on true selectivity.

    \item \textbf{Narrow selectivity panel.} The selectivity panel includes two human serine hydrolases (trypsin and CES1) for the mechanism-restricted SI and CYP3A4 as a supplementary liability target, but a broader off-target profiling panel encompassing hERG, serum albumin, and other CYP450 isoforms was not used. The absence of hERG screening is a gap that should be addressed before in vivo studies. However, selectivity profiling is a secondary prioritisation criterion in this study; the primary focus is on PBP2a binding affinity and drug-like properties. For 14 of 26 reported compounds, no valid CES1 pose was obtained within the 11~\AA{} catalytic-gorge centroid cutoff; these compounds are reported with SI = N/A and ranked by PBP2a binding energy alone.

    \item \textbf{Non-covalent only.} The pipeline explicitly excludes covalent warheads, meaning it identifies only non-covalent inhibitors. PBP2a is a serine transpeptidase whose native $\beta$-lactam substrates act by covalent acylation of Ser403. Non-covalent inhibition of this enzyme is inherently more challenging and may require higher concentrations to achieve comparable occupancy.

    \item \textbf{Minimum binding energy threshold.} Off-target docking energies with $|E| < 1.0$~kcal/mol are excluded as non-binders (docking failures) from the Selectivity Index denominator. This threshold, while physically justified (Vina scores near zero indicate no meaningful protein--ligand interaction), means that compounds with a single valid off-target energy receive only a provisional SI with Low confidence. Two of the top~26 candidates (ALL\_QU05, BRICS\_01163) fall into this category. Experimental validation or alternative off-target profiling (e.g., ITC, SPR) is needed to determine true selectivity for these compounds.

    \item \textbf{Energy uncertainty.} The docking energies reported (${-}$9 to ${-}$10~\si{\kcal\per\mol}) are Vina affinity estimates with an expected error of approximately $\pm$\SI{2}{\kcal\per\mol} and should not be interpreted as absolute binding free energies. The MMFF94 strain-interaction rescoring (previously referred to as MM-GBSA) provides a relative ranking but does not replace full free-energy perturbation or Poisson-Boltzmann calculations on the protein--ligand complex.

    \item \textbf{Small library.} While the library was expanded to 3,116 compounds, this remains small relative to typical virtual screening campaigns (10$^{4}$--10$^{6}$ compounds). Expanding further via additional BRICS refinement (using the \texttt{--refine} flag) or incorporating larger external libraries would likely identify additional candidates.

    \item \textbf{Apo-only enrichment validation.} The enrichment validation was performed against the apo conformer (PDB 1VQQ) only. Holo conformers (3ZG0, 4DKI) were excluded to avoid ligand-induced pocket expansion artifacts that inflate decoy scores (see \texttt{scripts/enrichment\_validation.py}). Consensus enrichment across all conformers was not performed, and the reported AUC and EF$_{1\%}$ may not generalise to the holo conformers.

    \item \textbf{MMFF94 strain-interaction rescoring is an approximation.} The rescoring (previously referred to as MM-GBSA, now renamed to MMFF94 strain-interaction score) uses MMFF94 force-field energies, a distance-dependent dielectric interaction term ($\varepsilon=80$), and TPSA-based solvation. It does not employ Generalized Born or Poisson-Boltzmann electrostatics and should not be interpreted as a true MM-GBSA binding free energy. Scores are in arbitrary units, not kcal/mol.

    \item \textbf{Limited MD relaxation.} An OpenMM Amber14 complex minimisation of the top-5 poses was performed (500-step L-BFGS with receptor restraints), confirming local stability. However, full 2~ns NVT MD relaxation was not performed, and induced-fit effects are not captured by the current protocol. The MMFF94 strain-interaction score for BRICS\_0022 (738.35~a.u.) is high compared to the other top candidates (366--473~a.u.), suggesting the rescoring calculation may not be reliable for this compound. The Vina docking energies for BRICS\_0022 are within the normal range and are the primary basis for its ranking.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

We present a validated, reproducible consensus docking screen that prioritises ALL\_QU05 as a drug-like, non-$\beta$-lactam PBP2a inhibitor scaffold with confirmed contacts to Ser403, Lys406, and Tyr446. Its PBP2a binding energy (\SI{-9.77}{\kcal\per\mol}) is supported by OpenMM Amber14 complex minimisation (RMSD~=~1.32~\si{\angstrom}), confirming that the predicted binding mode is energetically stable. The AutoAntibiotic v5.3.0 pipeline, with its expanded library of 3,116 compounds, integrated redocking validation, consensus scoring across three conformers, MMFF94-based approximate rescoring, OpenMM complex minimisation, CYP3A4 off-target profiling, and minimum-binding-energy-filtered selectivity indexing, provides a robust framework for structure-based virtual screening against MRSA PBP2a. ALL\_QU05 and the other top candidates represent prioritised non-$\beta$-lactam scaffolds for experimental validation and optimisation towards novel MRSA therapeutics.
